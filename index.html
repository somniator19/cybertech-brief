Overview

We are in Problem Discovery week. We have researched several cybersecurity problems and narrowed them to a shortlist. The goal of these technical sessions is to understand which problems are most acute, which are realistic for a small student team to tackle, and what a realistic MVP could look like.

Start with comparative scoring (1–5), then go deep on only the top 1–2 problems.
Priority

Legacy IoT / OT Security

Old industrial and IoT devices (cameras, PLCs, SCADA, routers) often cannot be patched, are poorly visible on the network, and are actively used by attackers. Direction: protect the network around the devices — inventory, risk scoring, isolation and monitoring — instead of trying to patch the devices themselves.

Why it hurts

  • Many organizations have no current list of OT/IoT devices.
  • Devices stay in service for 10–20 years with ended vendor support.
  • Default passwords, unencrypted protocols, and flat networks remain common.

Who feels the pain

  • Energy, water, mining, manufacturing, hospitals
  • OT/IT leads and plant engineers
  • Smaller factories and utilities underserved by enterprise tools
Recommended focus: industrial / critical infrastructure visibility (Option A).

Deepfake & Money Fraud

AI voice and video clones are used for financial fraud. A few seconds of audio are enough.

Why it hurts

  • Clone quality is high while human detection stays unreliable.
  • Urgency + social engineering bypasses normal approval checks.
  • Documented losses already run into the billions globally.

Who feels the pain

  • Banks, telecoms, and corporate finance teams
  • SMBs and older adults
  • Insurers facing rising claims
Possible angles: real-time detection, especially inside a telecom network, and continuous authentication that does not depend on “I recognize the voice.”

LLM Application Protection

Companies ship chatbots, RAG systems, and AI agents faster than they secure them. In the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2026, prompt injection stays at #1 and excessive agency has risen sharply.

Why it hurts

  • Prompt injection (direct and indirect)
  • Sensitive data leakage through retrieval and tools
  • Agents taking unintended real-world actions

Who feels the pain

  • Mid-to-large enterprises rolling out internal AI tools
  • Banks, telecoms, and software companies
  • CTOs, CISOs, and AI/ML engineering teams
Opportunity space: runtime monitoring, simplified red-teaming, and guardrails for teams without a large AppSec budget.

Backups & Tamper-Proof Logging

Attackers deliberately corrupt logs and backups so investigation and recovery fail. Direction: cryptographic anchoring — Merkle roots with off-chain storage, or blockchain-based integrity — for logs and backup states.

Why it hurts

  • Recovery rates after ransomware keep slipping despite more awareness.
  • Local logs and shared backup credentials are first targets.
  • Regulators expect non-repudiable audit trails.

Who feels the pain

  • CISOs, SOC and forensics teams
  • Regulated industries (finance, healthcare, critical infrastructure)
  • MSPs that must prove integrity to clients
Opportunity space: lightweight log/backup anchoring that plugs into existing SIEM pipelines without forcing full on-chain storage.

API Misuse

Incomplete API inventories, weak object-level authorization, overly broad permissions, and little monitoring of how APIs are actually used. The surface grows with every AI agent and internal service.

Why it hurts

  • Broken object-level authorization and excessive data exposure
  • Shared or long-lived API keys blur ownership
  • AI agents multiply endpoints faster than security reviews

Who feels the pain

  • Product and engineering teams
  • Banks, fintech, and e-commerce
  • Security teams without specialized API tooling
Opportunity space: continuous API discovery + risk scoring for smaller engineering teams that cannot afford a full API gateway suite.
Supporting

Quantum / Post-Quantum Cryptography

Not a standalone main problem. Realistic for this team: cryptographic inventory, and a quantum-risk layer on top of the legacy IoT/OT direction. Pure QKD and building quantum computers are low priority.

Why it hurts (long-term)

  • “Harvest now, decrypt later” already targets long-lived secrets.
  • Legacy devices often cannot adopt new algorithms.
  • Most organizations still lack a cryptographic inventory.

Who feels the pain

  • Governments, defense, and critical infrastructure
  • Teams protecting data with a long shelf life
  • Anyone preparing for PQC migration deadlines
Framing: the tool shows not only which devices are vulnerable today, but which ones will not survive a post-quantum transition.

Deep questions

Tap the number to cross out a question you already covered.

0 / 12 covered
  1. For Legacy IoT/OT visibility — is a purely passive network-listening approach realistic as an MVP, or do we need some form of agent or active scanning from the start?
  2. What are practical ways to fingerprint and identify unknown industrial/IoT devices without having signatures for every vendor?
  3. How should we prioritise devices (CVEs + exposure + criticality of the process they control)?
  4. In real industrial environments, how do companies actually isolate risky legacy devices without breaking production? What usually goes wrong?
  5. Given our current skill mix, which technical direction is most realistic to prototype in 4–6 weeks?
  6. What is the smallest useful thing we could show as an MVP (discovery + inventory dashboard, risk report, something else)?
  7. Are there open datasets, traffic captures or lab environments you would recommend for testing OT/IoT discovery ideas?
  8. For deepfake voice detection — what approaches can realistically run with low latency (edge / network-level)?
  9. For LLM application security — where do you still see meaningful gaps that a small team could address?
  10. Which two problems could realistically be combined into one stronger solution?
  11. For which problem is it easiest to quickly validate reality with 5 interviews in one week?
  12. Who should we talk to next, and could you introduce us?
